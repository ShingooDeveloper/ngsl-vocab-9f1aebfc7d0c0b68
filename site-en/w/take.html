take

/teɪk/verb, noun

irregular verb: take – took – taken

  1. verbto move someone or something from one place to another, or carry it with you
    • Please take the folder that is on my desk to the meeting room.
    • He was taken to the hospital right after the accident happened.
    • Take a torch with you before you go into the dark cave at night.
  2. verbto need a particular amount of time
    • It takes about twenty minutes to walk from here to the station.
    • It takes a while to mine enough diamonds to craft a full set of armor.
  3. verbto use a bus, train, taxi, or road to go somewhere
    • If I took a taxi, I would get to the airport much faster.
  4. verbto make a photograph with a camera
    • I've taken a lot of photos on this trip, but I want more.
  5. nounyour opinion about something, or the way you understand it
    • What's your take on this issue?
Usage: For the 'need time' meaning, use it as the subject: it takes (someone) + time + to do something. Say "It takes me ten minutes to get there," not "I take ten minutes to get there."

Collocations

take a break — to stop working for a short rest
take a look at — to look at something quickly

Word family

mistake — a wrong action, answer, or idea

overtake — to pass a person or vehicle that is moving in front of you
intake — the amount of something that is taken in

retake — to take a test or an exam again

Synonyms

bring — to carry something toward the speaker or listener (the opposite direction to take)
get — to obtain or receive something; the focus is on the result
carry — to hold and move something while you go

Phrasal verbs

take off — (of a plane) to leave the ground and start to fly; also to remove clothesThe plane took off from Narita right on time.
take over — to take control of something, such as a job or a companyShe will take over the family business next year.
take up — to start a hobby or an activity; to fill an amount of space or timeHe took up golf after he retired last spring.
take out — to remove something from a place; to carry rubbish outsidePlease take out the trash before you leave home.

Idioms

take place — to happen, especially after being plannedThe wedding will take place at a small church next spring.
take care of — to look after someone or something, or deal with a taskCould you take care of the kids while I make dinner?
take advantage of — to use a chance or situation well; also, to treat someone unfairly for your own gainWe should take advantage of the sunny weather and go hiking.
take part in — to join an activity together with other peopleOver a hundred students took part in the volunteer program.
take A into account — to think about something when you make a decisionYou should take the weather into account when planning the trip.
take it easy — to relax and not work too hard; also, to stay calmYou've been working too hard—take it easy this weekend.
take your time — to do something slowly and carefully, without hurryingThere's no rush at all—please take your time deciding.
take turns — to do something one after another, each person in orderThe two brothers take turns washing the dishes every night.
take A for granted — to not show that you are thankful for something because you are used to having itWe often take clean water for granted until it runs out.
take effect — (of a law or a drug) to start to work or to become officialThe new tax rules take effect at the start of next month.
